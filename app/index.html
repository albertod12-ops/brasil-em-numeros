Brazil by the numbers

One page, six years, every figure sourced: 212.6 million people, a R$11.7 trillion economy, inflation back inside target and the lowest unemployment in a decade. Pick a year — the whole page follows it.

Year in focus:

Real GDP growth, 2019–2024

The pandemic cut GDP by 3.3% in 2020; the rebound peaked at 5.0% in 2021, and growth has run at 3% or better for three straight years — 3.4% in 2024.

Source: IBGE, national accounts, annual real GDP growth.

Consumer inflation (IPCA), 2019–2024

Inflation spiked to 10.06% in 2021, then fell back inside the central bank's target band: 4.83% in 2024.

Source: IBGE, IPCA, annual average change.

Population, 2019–2024

The 2022 Census counted 203.08 million people — well below the old estimates — so the series steps down in 2022 and the 2023–24 figures are post-census estimates.

Source: IBGE population estimates; 2022 Census. The census revision is why the 2022 point sits below its neighbours.

Economy

Latin America's largest economy: R$11.66 trillion in 2024, about US$2.17 trillion — the world's ninth-largest by nominal GDP.

Nominal GDP, 2019–2024

Nominal GDP in reais rose every year of the series, from R$7.39 trillion in 2019 to R$11.66 trillion in 2024. In dollars it fell in 2020 as the real weakened, then recovered.

Source: IBGE, national accounts, trillion reais.

Where exports go, 2024

Exports hit a record US$337.0 billion; imports were US$262.9 billion, leaving a US$74.1 billion surplus.

Source: MDIC, Comex Stat, 2024.

Money & rates

The Selic policy rate ended 2024 at 12.25% after the central bank resumed hikes in September. GDP per capita reached US$10,242.

12.25% Selic · end 2024

US$10,242 GDP per capita · 2024

Services are about 70% of GDP; agriculture about 7% and industry about 23%. Soybeans, crude oil and iron ore lead exports.

Source: Banco Central do Brasil; IBGE; IMF WEO, October 2025.

Prices & work

Unemployment fell every year from 2021: 6.6% in 2024, the lowest annual average since the PNAD series began in 2012.

Unemployment, 2019–2024

The jobless rate peaked at 13.5% in 2020, stayed high through 2021, then dropped fast — 9.3% in 2022, 7.8% in 2023, 6.6% in 2024. Informal work still covers about 38% of workers.

Source: IBGE, PNAD Contínua, annual average unemployment rate.

The labour market in 2024

Formal employment hit a record: 47.2 million workers with a signed labour card (carteira assinada) at year-end, and the real average wage rose 3.6% on the year.
